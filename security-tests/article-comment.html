Field Notes: Urban Mesh Networks

Community mesh networks route traffic peer-to-peer across rooftop radios instead of relying on a single carrier backbone. In dense neighborhoods, a few dozen nodes can provide resilient local connectivity that survives outages in the commercial grid.

The trade-offs are latency and maintenance: each hop adds delay, and volunteer-run nodes need occasional reflashing. Still, for disaster resilience and digital sovereignty, mesh remains one of the more quietly radical pieces of infrastructure a community can build for itself.

Practical starting points are inexpensive single-board radios, a shared addressing scheme, and a rooftop with line-of-sight to two or three neighbors.
